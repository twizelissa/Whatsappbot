The aim is to clearly understand who experiences the problem, what the problem is, why it happens, and why it matters. A good problem statement should be short, begin with the affected person or group, explain the problem and its root cause, and avoid describing your product or solution. It should also address a problem that customers would be willing to pay to solve.

The module includes four lessons and three venture activities:

1. Identify the problem your startup solves, including supporting data and its source. The statement should be no more than 350 characters.
2. Select the area, industry and domain that best match your idea.
3. Describe who is affected, how they are affected, the root causes, why the problem matters to you, and the negative impact on each stakeholder. Then restate the problem in 150 characters.

The session also emphasised identifying your primary customer first if several groups are affected, and remembering that the person who benefits may not be the person who pays. You can revise the activities as many times as needed; nothing is final at this stage.
